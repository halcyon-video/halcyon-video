# Paperboard cartons and season slipboxes

Original scripted Blender meshes augment the existing packaging family. Source is
`tools/models/packaging-extras.blend`; regenerate all four exports with Blender in
background mode and `tools/models/packaging-extras.py`. The script imports the
existing packaging profile helpers and native glTF exporter. Old exports are not
regenerated by that import. Collections distinguish stock and inspected variants.
There are no external meshes, textures, cover-art scans, cartridges or discs.

## Construction and attachment contract

The game carton is a thin folded sleeve with a glue lap and fitted closed end
flaps. The season sleeve is a continuous thin-walled slipbox, closed at its printed
spine and open on the right. Four recessed case ends articulate that opening;
concealed inner cases and discs are deliberately absent. These are generic
construction families, not newly measured regional retail editions.

Store units remain feet, centered on every axis: front +Z, spine -X, opening +X.
Blender uses (x, -z, height) and the native Y-up exporter. Closed dimensions and
attachment datums are unchanged. Known paperboard platforms select game-carton;
unknown and ambiguous platforms retain their previous fallback. The inherited
6.6-inch width cap continues to fit wide cartons uniformly. Rental geometry is
independent and retains its exact bottom alignment with each retail family.

Non-streaming series use series-boxset in aisle, back-wall and fixture batches,
with the existing 3.5 depth multiplier applied once. Inspected series use the same
construction at their full depth. Episode/season grouping and generated panels
are unchanged. Streaming titles keep their established paired-card presentation.

All six existing artwork material lanes and their UV handedness are retained.
The GLB loader retains ownership, loading fallback, cache and disposal behavior.
New assets contain no baked lettering or colors that bypass the live artwork.

## Measured cost and evidence

Each carton export has 72 triangles in three closed solid parts, 10,744 bytes for
stock and 10,760 bytes for inspection. Each slipbox has 76 triangles in five closed
solid parts, 12,824 bytes for stock and 12,864 bytes for inspection. The shared
loader combines them into the six existing material lanes. No textures are added.

The authoring exporter checks closed topology and positive volume. Export tests
check finite normals/UVs, the declared bounds, closed carton flaps, recessed case
ends and the physical gaps between them. Runtime ray checks compare all six
carton artwork orientations against the previous box convention for NES, SNES,
Game Boy, Super Famicom and Nintendo 64 in both movie media; rental bottom lifts
and series depths are checked independently. Actual store stock and inspected
front, back and opening views are recorded in the private publicity folder.
